# Bike-MRT

Fastest door-to-door commute planner for a **trifold bicycle + MRT/LRT** in Singapore. Pick a start and a destination and it finds the best mix of cycling and trains, including trips like *cycle → MRT → cycle → MRT → cycle*, and shows:

- total time, cycling km/minutes and rail minutes,
- every leg on a map (cycle routes along real roads and park connectors, rail along the real track),
- the MRT/LRT lines, stations and transfers,
- **turn-by-turn navigation** ("Start navigation"): on the cycling legs the map follows your GPS position with the next turn, the street name and the distance, and warns you when you are off the route, with a re-route button; at the station it switches to **step cards for the train ride** that you page through with the left and right arrows (enter at this exit, take this line towards X, change here, get off and leave by that exit), and then carries on cycling; when you arrive it downloads **one activity file for Strava** with your real GPS track of the cycling parts (the train rides are paused time),
- an **intro screen** explaining how to use the app, with an "add to home screen" prompt where the browser supports it,
- **search by building, business or station name** (merges MRT/LRT stations, [OneMap](https://www.onemap.gov.sg/) for buildings, landmarks and postal codes, and [Photon](https://photon.komoot.io/) for OpenStreetMap shops, cafes, clinics and other businesses), with a clear button and keyboard support,
- **which station exit to use** for every train ride ("Enter at Exit A · Leave at Exit 4"), drawn on the map, with the cycling routed to and from that exact exit,
- **estimated MRT/LRT fares** per train ride and per plan (adult, student or senior/disability card fares),
- a button to reset the map view, and on phones a draggable bottom sheet so the map can fill almost the whole screen,
- a **settings page**: light / dark / auto theme (dark mode also darkens the map), cycling speed, longest single ride, cycling between stations on/off, "prefer trains over cycling", pick which MRT/LRT lines to use, a departure time (peak or off-peak is worked out from the time and day), fold and unfold times, extra time per line change, avoid-transfers, number of plans (saved on your device),
- a feedback button (opens an email),
- a map limited to Singapore, with pins and GPS accepted only on Singapore land,
- **starts from your GPS location by default**: the app asks for your location when it opens and sets "My location" as the start. When you plan, or start navigating, it takes a fresh reading, and if you have moved more than 80 m since the plan was made it plans again from where you are. You can still type another start, drop a pin, or switch the behaviour off in Settings (*Start from my location*),
- **favourite destinations**: tap ☆ next to the destination to save it under a name (for example Home or Work). Favourites are shown on the home page; one tap on a favourite sets the destination and shows the routes. *Edit* removes them. They are kept on your device (up to 20).

Everything runs **in your browser**: there is no backend. It is a static site that can be hosted for free (GitHub Pages) and installed on a phone as an app (PWA: open the site, then "Add to Home Screen").

It has not had a line-by-line human code review, so treat the routes and times as estimates, check them against the map, and please open an issue or PR if you find a bug.

## How it works

1. **Bike router** (`frontend/js/router.js`): Dijkstra over a compact Singapore road graph exported from OpenStreetMap, with a trifold-bicycle speed cap (16 km/h), a preference for cycleways and quiet roads, and one-way rules.
2. **Planner** (`frontend/js/planner.js`): a shortest-path search over a combined graph of bike and rail states. Switching mode costs fold/unfold and waiting time, and transfers cost extra, so any number of bike ↔ rail alternations can come out of it. It returns the fastest plan plus lower-cycling alternatives.
3. **Data** (`frontend/data/`) is generated by the scripts below from the OSM extract. Rail times are estimates from distance (not timetables).

## Run locally

```
frontend> python -m http.server 8000     # then open http://localhost:8000
```

(or run `start.bat` on Windows). Map tiles and address search need an internet connection.

## Rebuild the data

Needs Python 3.11+ (`pip install osmium numpy`) and Node 20+. Download `Singapore.osm.pbf` from [BBBike](https://download.bbbike.org/osm/bbbike/Singapore/) into `data/`, then:

```
python extract_stations.py     # MRT/LRT station positions       -> data/stations_raw.json
python build_mrt.py            # stations + links + travel times -> data/stations.json, mrt_edges.json
python build_shapes.py         # snap links onto real track       -> data/mrt_shapes.json
python build_entrances.py      # station exits (Exit A, B, C...)  -> frontend/data/entrances.json
python build_land.py           # Singapore land outline (map limits, pin checks) -> frontend/data/sg_land.json
python export_bikegraph.py     # compact bike graph (keeps the strongly connected core, so every pin can be left and entered) + street names for the directions -> frontend/data/bg_*.bin, names.json
copy data\stations.json data\mrt_edges.json data\mrt_shapes.json frontend\data\
node build_matrix.mjs          # station-to-station cycling times -> frontend/data/bike_matrix.json
python compress_data.py        # gzip the graph for publishing
```

Bump `VERSION` in `frontend/sw.js` after changing data so installed apps refresh their cache.

## Releasing a new version

Bump the build id in three places so an update never mixes old and new files: `<meta name="build">` in `frontend/index.html`, `BUILD` in `frontend/js/app.js` and `VERSION` in `frontend/sw.js` (`node tests/version.test.mjs` checks they match). If a phone ever ends up with mismatched files, the app shows a *Reload latest version* banner.

## Deploy

Pushing to `main` publishes `frontend/` to GitHub Pages through `.github/workflows/pages.yml`.

## How rail times are estimated

There is no public timetable or transfer-time dataset for the MRT/LRT, so the app builds estimates from what *is* published:

- **Ride time between stops:** straight-line distance between stations (from OpenStreetMap) at an average train speed plus a short stop time. MRT: 55 km/h and 0.5 min per stop; LRT: 28 km/h with a winding-track factor. Checked against known end-to-end times (East-West Line about 79 min).
- **Waiting for a train:** half the gap between trains, using LTA's published frequencies: [2-3 min at peak and about 5 min off-peak, Thomson-East Coast Line 5 and 6 min](https://lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2024/9/media-replies/public-transport---keeping-it-convenient--affordable.html). LRT is assumed at 3 / 6 min. Peak is worked out from the departure time (now, or one you pick, in Singapore time): weekdays 7-9 am and 5-7 pm. Public holidays are not known to the app.
- **Changing lines:** walking time plus the wait for the next train on the new line. Walking defaults to 3 min. [City Hall](https://landtransportguru.net/city-hall-station/), Raffles Place, Jurong East and Tanah Merah (Changi Airport branch) are cross-platform (1 min). Tampines (about 300 m linkway, 6.5 min) and [Newton](https://landtransportguru.net/newton-station/) (56 m linkway, 4 min) take longer, and the LRT at Bukit Panjang is about 200 m from the Downtown Line (4 min). These are estimates, and you can add or remove minutes per change in Settings.
- **Boarding:** folding the bike, the gates and walking to the platform (2.5 min by default) plus the wait for the train. Leaving the system costs 2 min to exit and unfold.

## Plans

The list shows the fastest plans that use the MRT/LRT (as many as you choose in Settings) and, **always as the last option, *Cycle all the way***: the whole trip by bike, whatever the "longest single ride" setting says. If cycling the whole way is the fastest of all, it is labelled *Cycle all the way · fastest*.

## Navigation

Tapping *Start navigation* walks through the plan leg by leg.

- **Cycling legs:** the turn-by-turn directions are worked out in the browser from the route: at each junction the change of heading (measured over about 12 m before and after it) decides *turn left / bear right / U-turn*, junctions closer than 30 m are merged (roundabouts, zig-zags) and the street name comes from OpenStreetMap (unnamed paths say *the cycle path* or *the footpath*). Your GPS position is projected onto the route to get the distance to the next turn and the time left. After three fixes more than 45 m (or the GPS accuracy) away from the route you are told you are off route and can re-plan from where you are.
- **Train rides:** GPS rarely works underground, so the train part is a set of cards you move through yourself with the arrows (or by swiping, or the keyboard arrows). The direction on each line ("towards Pasir Ris") comes from the station numbers along the ride.
- **The screen stays on** (Wake Lock) while navigating, where the browser supports it.
- Everything runs on the phone, so navigation keeps working with a weak signal once the map has loaded (map tiles themselves need data).

## Strava file

While you navigate, the phone's real GPS fixes of the **cycling legs** are recorded (one point a second, fixes worse than 50 m accuracy are skipped). When you press *Arrived* (or arrive automatically) the app downloads one file, `Bike-MRT_<date>_<time>.fit`:

- **FIT** is the format Garmin and Wahoo devices write, so it can say "paused": the timer stops when you reach the station and starts again when you continue cycling, the distance counter does not move during the train ride (no giant jump from station to station), each cycling leg is a lap, and the sport is cycling. Strava therefore shows your moving time without the MRT ride.
- **Commute:** the FIT sub-sport is set to *commuting* (code 48), which Strava turns into its **Commute** tag.
- **Title:** the title is `Commute: <From> → <To>` (taken from the From and To fields when the trip starts). It is in the file name and in the GPX. I found no evidence that Strava reads a title from inside a FIT file, so the arrival card also shows the title with a **Copy title** button to paste on the upload page.
- The positions are the actual GPS track, not the planned route.
- A **GPX** version (one track segment per cycling leg) is available on the arrival card as a fallback; GPX has no pause concept.
- Upload at strava.com/upload, or share the file to the Strava app. Strava replaces the elevation with its own data.
- If you press *End* early, the app asks whether to save the ride so far.

The FIT writer is a small dependency-free encoder (`frontend/js/activity.js`). Its output is checked with an independent parser in `tests/activity.test.mjs`, and was also decoded with the Python `fitdecode` and `fitparse` libraries (CRC verified). It has not been uploaded to Strava from this repository's tests, so tell me if Strava rejects a file.

## Station exits

Exits come from OpenStreetMap (`railway=subway_entrance` nodes with their letter or number), assigned to the nearest station: about 490 exits at 133 of the 190 stations (LRT stations and some MRT stations are not mapped with exits yet, and those simply show no exit). For each train ride the planner picks the exit that is **quickest to cycle to** from where you come from when boarding, and the exit **quickest to cycle away from** towards your next stop or destination when leaving, using the same bike router, and then draws the cycling leg to and from that exact exit. The data can be incomplete or out of date (closed exits, renumbering), so always check the signs in the station. Which train carriage or door to stand at is not published as open data, so the app does not suggest that.

## Fares

Fares come from LTA's [MRT/LRT fare tables](https://sbstransit.com.sg/fares-and-concessions) (effective 27 Dec 2025): adult, student and senior/disability card fares by distance, with the weekday before-7:45-am discount. The app switches automatically to the [2026 fare review](https://mothership.sg/2026/09/fare-adjustment-2026/) fares from 26 Dec 2026 (adult +12 / +13 cents, concession +5 cents). The distance is the estimated track length, so a fare can be one band (about 5-10 cents) off. Every train ride is priced as its own journey, because cycling in between breaks the trip. Workfare, cash fares and bus fares are not included.

## Limitations

- Turn instructions are generated from OpenStreetMap geometry, not by a commercial navigation engine, so they can occasionally be odd at complicated junctions or on unmapped paths. Keep your eyes on the road and use common sense.
- The browser only gives a position while the page is open and the screen is on; the app asks the phone to keep the screen awake while you navigate. Other apps in the foreground will pause position updates.

- Train times are estimates; there is no live disruption, crowding or timetable data. LRT loops are treated as running in both directions, although off-peak some loops run one way only.
- Cycling routes come from OpenStreetMap and can differ from what is legal or comfortable on the ground. Always check the route on the map.
- Fold-and-wait, unfold and transfer times are adjustable in Settings.
- Not affiliated with LTA, SMRT, SBS Transit or Google.
